# Aphantasia association projects: feasibility and novelty audit

Research checked 10 September 2026; source-file and pipeline status updated 14 September 2026. Priorities: existing participant data, a credible access route, and a question that adds meaningfully to published work. Journal suggestions below are editorial-fit judgments, not acceptance predictions. See [the dataset register](DATA_SOURCES.md), [analysis runbook](ANALYSIS_RUNBOOK.md), and [first-pass results](RESULTS_SUMMARY.md) for what has now been staged and run.

## 1. Prospective imagery–mental-health associations in NZAVS

**Question:** Does baseline visual imagery predict subsequent psychological distress or wellbeing after accounting for the baseline outcome? Does auditory imagery contribute independently?

The 2026 paper uses imagery questions from Time 13 of the New Zealand Attitudes and Values Study. It includes 32,784 respondents, including 261 answering that they cannot visualise. Its reported associations are concurrent; simply repeating aphantasia–distress, wellbeing, personality or rumination correlations would duplicate existing work. Imagery was measured with single items, not the full VVIQ. [Original paper](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuropsychologia.2026.109527).

**Proposed contribution:** Prespecify a primary later-wave outcome and estimate its association with T13 imagery while adjusting for the same outcome at T13. Model the full imagery scale flexibly; use the no-imagery group as a secondary contrast. Account for auditory imagery, plausible pre-exposure confounders and loss to follow-up. This is observational prediction, not evidence that imagery causes or prevents mental illness.

**Access:** New collaboration with NZAVS leadership. Later waves exist, but exact later-wave K6/wellbeing/rumination availability, matched sample size, and permission for a novel project require confirmation. Do not assume imagery was measured repeatedly. The data-request language in some published studies covers replication/checking and does not automatically authorize new analyses. [NZAVS official information](https://www.psych.auckland.ac.nz/en/about/new-zealand-attitudes-and-values-study.html), [public project materials](https://osf.io/75SNB/), [published access statement and longitudinal rumination measures](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2023.1239112/full).

**Go/no-go:** Confirm a new-project collaboration, the relevant linked outcome fields, retention of the no-imagery subgroup, and that the team has not already planned this analysis. With those confirmed, this is the strongest substantive association project in this shortlist. Potential journals: Neuropsychologia; Journal of Affective Disorders if the clinical question and outcome coverage justify it.

## 2. Why do autism–aphantasia findings disagree?

**Question:** How much do imagery definitions, autism ascertainment, questionnaire content and recruitment explain differences between published estimates?

Taylor et al. (2026) report an association and elevated aphantasia in their autistic group; Arnold et al. (2026), which recruited 327 people reporting a formal autism diagnosis plus a comparison group, find no elevated prevalence of visual aphantasia. These are not interchangeable samples or measures. [Taylor paper](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-026-38574-9), [Arnold paper](https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/id/eprint/10230162/).

**Proposed contribution:** A preregistered synthesis using participant-level data where obtainable. Analyze diagnostic group comparisons separately from dimensional trait correlations. Estimate study-specific associations, compare absence versus low imagery definitions, and assess common measurement items and recruitment differences. Do not standardize unrelated questionnaires and call them equivalent. Do not infer causal recruitment effects from a small number of studies.

**Already done:** Removing imagination questions from the Autism Spectrum Quotient, testing visual versus other imagery modalities, and simple diagnosis-by-imagery comparisons are not sufficient novelty by themselves. Bouyer et al. already examine imagination/social-skills content. [Bouyer paper](https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/10205390/1/Bouyer%20et%20al%202025.pdf). A first-pass reanalysis of Arnold's public code is now staged; it is not, by itself, the proposed multi-study synthesis.

**Access:** Arnold provides a UQ repository route; Taylor, King and Bouyer require checking/requesting the relevant data. Do not sum sample sizes until shared controls and repeated participation are resolved. [Arnold dataset](https://doi.org/10.48610/57f325b), [King paper](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1053810024001168).

Arnold's analyzed sample comprises 327 autistic respondents and 327 comparison respondents (654 total). The comparison cohort came from an earlier study; its provenance needs checking. AQ/CATI were collected only in the autistic group, which limits whole-sample trait analyses. Taylor's raw data are a particularly important request because its VVIQ-based result is central to the apparent disagreement.

**Go/no-go:** Obtain several independent datasets with enough overlapping measures to support the specific comparison. A one-dataset rerun is weak. Potential journals: Autism Research, Consciousness and Cognition; Cortex would require a compelling general measurement contribution.

## 3. Public-data fallback: visual-specific versus broader imagery associations

New et al. (2026) studied 1,952 participants across two samples (949 and 1,003), measuring eight imagery modalities. Study 2 additionally includes emotional imagery and a neurodevelopmental-condition question combining diagnosis with strong suspicion. The paper already reports imagery profiles and broad condition associations. [Paper](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cortex.2026.04.019), [authors' public data link](https://osf.io/2bdn6/).

**Possible extension:** Compare a general cross-modal imagery association with a direct conditional visual/nonvisual model, asking whether visual imagery adds information beyond other modalities and whether a broad imagery composite carries the association. Establish the measurement structure before interpreting differences between scales. Prefer a small prespecified model over exploratory clusters followed by dozens of outcome tests. The public files are now staged and the published attention-check Ns are reproduced; see [the first-pass results](RESULTS_SUMMARY.md). The questionnaire crosswalk for `diag` is confirmed; estimates remain exploratory until the novelty and final estimand are frozen.

Both samples include the same confirmed current-treatment item, so that association can be estimated in one sample and checked in the other. Treatment status is not equivalent to a psychiatric diagnosis or symptom severity. The direct visual coefficient is primary; common-imagery-shift and visual-minus-nonvisual contrasts are reported from that same model. The joint study interaction does not show a clear study difference, while Study 2 is less precise. The dataset supports a careful, transparent secondary analysis rather than a simple positive replication claim. NZAVS remains the stronger prospective opportunity if access is granted.

**Limitations:** The neurodevelopmental outcome is broad and combines diagnosed and suspected conditions; it cannot automatically support an ADHD-specific or diagnosis-confirmed conclusion. Its main analysis is restricted to Study 2, not all 1,952 participants. The file-level QC audit and questionnaire crosswalk are complete; overlap with analyses in the source paper still requires the novelty table. Potential journals: Consciousness and Cognition or Collabra: Psychology if the final contribution is distinct and measured carefully.

## Other leads and why they are not first choices

- **Mental healthcare survey:** Mawtus et al. provide an author-declared public dataset of 2,815 respondents, including 2,405 aphants and 410 controls. It includes seven-modality PSI-Q and healthcare/intrusion questions. However, the paper already examines intrusion modalities, visual/nonvisual therapy, CBT, and condition-specific perceived effectiveness. A more precise imagery-capacity-by-symptom-modality analysis might add value, but needs raw-field and cell-count checks. ADHD appears in open-text responses rather than a systematic diagnostic assessment, and congenital/acquired status was not collected. [Paper](https://doi.org/10.1525/collabra.127416), [data](https://osf.io/v7krh/).
- **Narrow therapy extension:** In the same Mawtus dataset, test whether a person's retained imagery capacity in a modality interacts with use of that modality in therapy to predict perceived effectiveness. This differs from merely comparing visual and nonvisual therapy. Proceed only if the fields link capacity, modality and effectiveness at a suitable level, with adequate cell counts. Retrospective treatment selection precludes a causal treatment-matching claim.
- **Alexithymia:** A 2026 pooled analysis already tests linear versus nonlinear associations and distinguishes completely absent from weak imagery across five studies, N=1,478. Repeating this is not a new project. [Paper and open-data statement](https://peercommunityjournal.org/articles/10.24072/pcjournal.772/).
- **ADHD:** An acquired-aphantasia study has ASRS-6 data, but its N=59 selected acquired sample cannot estimate whether ADHD is associated with lifelong aphantasia in the general population. [Original study](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0028393225002076).
- **Generation Scotland:** Dance et al. assessed 1,285 GS respondents with a single imagery question, not VVIQ. Linkability of those responses to the broader cohort's mental-health/genetic records is unconfirmed. The autism experiment in that paper is a separate sample. New GS applications are paused through the end of September 2026 according to its researcher page. [Dance paper](https://eagleman.com/papers/Danceetal_2021_AphantasiaAutismSynesthesia.pdf), [GS access](https://genscot.ed.ac.uk/for-researchers).
- **UK Biobank:** Its visualisation/memory questionnaire opened in November 2023 and includes multisensory imagery. ADHD/autism-related Social Interactions and Focus data have been released for over 160,000 people. This creates a potentially excellent future linkage opportunity, but imagery response release, field IDs and respondent overlap were not verified. The 160,000 figure must not be presented as an imagery-plus-ADHD analysis sample. [Imagery questionnaire](https://www.ukbiobank.ac.uk/taking-part/participant-opportunities/online-questionnaires/visualisation-and-memory-questionnaire/), [confirmed data release](https://community.ukbiobank.ac.uk/hc/en-gb/articles/26655455734301-Data-release-v20).
